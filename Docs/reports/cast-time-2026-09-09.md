# Abilities wind up

O266. An authored cast time delays the RESOLUTION of an ability, not the press. Mana goes on the keypress and is never returned, damage taken interrupts the cast, and an interrupted cast is a loss. Movement and defensive verbs author no wind-up, owner-ruled: a dodge with a wind-up is not a dodge.

## The shape, and why it is a gate rather than a refactor

`BeginCastIfNeeded` is one line at the top of an ability's `ActivateAbility`. It returns true when the ability should resolve now — no authored wind-up, or this is the re-entry after one — and false when it has started a cast, having already committed the cost, opened the window and set the timer that will call `ActivateAbility` again.

The alternative was splitting every ability into activate-and-resolve halves. The gate was chosen because every ability's body then stays exactly where it is and keeps its own commit, which is what makes the rule safe to apply across a whole class in one pass. The one piece of bookkeeping that buys is `bCastCommitted`: the re-entry runs the ability's own `CommitAbility`, and the base short-circuits it so the price is taken once.

The cast holds a `Window.Cast.<AbilityId>` window, which means the HUD's existing ability-window bar draws a pending cast with no HUD change at all — O179 already says a window is a HUD bar. The key is per-ability so two casts can never share a countdown.

`EffectiveCastSeconds` is the seam the Cast Speed lane plugs into and reads a multiplier of 1.0 today. That is deliberate rather than an omission: a new multiplier lane needs a canon row in `power-and-scaling.md` and a conformance test before any node or affix may bid into it, so the lane lands as one number instead of a rewrite.

## Coverage: 24 of 34, and the ten exceptions are four different things

The owner's rule is that every skill winds up except the ones that are self-evidently instant, so that Cast Speed has something to divide. Twenty-four abilities across Swift, Gunsmith, Tank and Support now carry one. The ten that do not are not one category:

**Instant by ruling, permanently.** Swift.Slipcut, Swift.HardStop, Caster.Closequarter, Tank.GroundZero. Movement and defensive verbs; Hard Stop's own header says it cancels velocity *instantly*, and a blink is movement.

**Already own their timing.** Caster.Fracture has a bespoke cast phase, Caster.Siphon is a five-second channel, and Tank.BreachCharge carries a 1.2 s fuse. A generic wind-up on top of a bespoke one casts the ability TWICE — Fracture's own phase test caught it emitting two projectiles where it asserts one. These want their existing phase reinterpreted as the cast time, not a second delay stacked on it.

**Parked on fixture architecture.** Caster.Rot and Caster.Resonance want a wind-up and cannot have one yet. Their runtime fixtures build worlds that never call `InitializeActorsForPlay`, so a timer-driven cast can never resolve there — and adding the call crashes them outright, because they were never written for a play world. That is a fixture rewrite rather than a data edit.

**Parked on a ruling.** Caster.Unmake opens the window that rewrites every Caster cost. Delaying that window moves the whole class's cost model, which is a design decision rather than a delay.

The irony worth fixing first: the Caster is the class the owner plays, and Cleave is still its only wind-up.

## How the fixtures were brought across

Authoring all thirty-four at once turned **34 tests red**. They are not wrong about their subjects — they are wrong about *when*, which is exactly what this ruling changes.

The tail was closed with one helper rather than thirty-four hand edits. `BreakerResolvePendingCast` advances a fixture's world by exactly the wind-up that is running and no further, because ticking a flat "long enough" interval would also advance every status, zone and cooldown those tests measure. Two things were learned building it. It must ask the ABILITY whether it is still casting, not the cast window: the window needs a ticking state component to expire, and a fixture that never registers one reports no window at all and exits before the cast resolves — that mistake alone left 33 of 34 still red. And it must refuse to tick a world that never had `InitializeActorsForPlay` called, because doing so crashes inside the engine's tick group setup; `AreActorsInitialized` is the question that distinguishes them, while `HasBegunPlay` is false in every one of these fixtures and silently disables the whole helper.

## What actually shipped, and what was reverted

**Cleave alone winds up, at 0.35s.** It is the owner's own report and the ability the rule ships on.

Rot at 0.60, Siphon at 0.50, Fracture at 0.70, Resonance at 0.80 and Unmake at 0.90 were authored, measured, and reverted inside this cycle. With all six live the suite turned **38 unexpected reds**; Cleave alone costs 6. Every one of those tests encodes "the effect lands on the press", which is precisely what this ruling changes — they are not wrong about their subjects, they are wrong about when. Fixing 38 fixtures is a block of its own and is on the desk as one. Shipping the mechanism with one ability proves it end to end and leaves the rest as scoped, mechanical work rather than a half-finished pass.

## The six fixtures Cleave did break, and what each one taught

Four needed only a clock advance past the wind-up, reading the number from the file rather than restating it, so a retune cannot silently un-prove them.

**Overreach** stopped reaching Mana debt at all. One Cleave now occupies its wind-up and then its animation lock, so repeat casts are spaced further apart, regeneration covers more of each one, and twenty casts no longer drive the bank negative. That is the wind-up doing exactly what the owner asked for — "you can just straight up spam spells super fast" — so the loop was given more repetitions rather than the rule being weakened.

**CleaveAcceptedHit** exposed the sharpest consequence in the batch. Parry is an earned Core verb rather than a class ability or a baseline one — Core.Bulwark.Parry grants Progression.Verb.Parry, and any class may buy it — with a base window of 0.25 s against a 0.35 s wind-up, so a parry pressed when the caster starts swinging has already lapsed by the time the arc lands. Core.Bulwark.Counterweight adds +0.10 s of window, which takes it to exactly 0.35 s and closes the gap precisely; that is a coincidence of two O2 placeholders, not a designed relationship. The defender must now react to the wind-up rather than pre-empt it. The fixture parries late, inside the window that actually covers the landing. This is arguably the point of a wind-up, but it is a real change to how parry feels and **nobody has played it** — it is on the desk for the owner's hands.

## Evidence

`RiorsEdge.Abilities.CastTime.Rule` pins the divisor convention (1.25 is a 20% shorter cast, never a 25% longer one), that an unauthored wind-up stays instant whatever the multiplier, that a malformed multiplier is floored rather than dividing by zero, and that the window key is per-ability and carries the HUD's prefix.

`RiorsEdge.Abilities.CastTime.Shipped` pins Cleave at 0.35 and pins Closequarter, Slipcut, Hard Stop, Cadence Break and Anchor Point at instant — Closequarter is a blink, so it is movement — and checks every registry row carries a finite, non-negative wind-up that agrees with its own predicate.

`RiorsEdge.Abilities.CastTime.InterruptRuntime` proves the half that is a risk, in a real world: the keypress pays before anything resolves, the wind-up runs as a live HUD window, a real hit landing a third of the way through stops the swing, the window is gone, and the Mana is not handed back. It carries a control — an undisturbed cast of the same ability does land — because without one the test would pass equally well against an ability that never worked at all.

## The fixture rewrite, and the two things it found instead

The owner asked for the fixture rewrite so Rot and Resonance could wind up rather than leaving Cast Speed with a dead pool to modify. The rewrite itself succeeded and unblocked neither, which is worth more than it sounds.

`BreakerCasterResourceNodeTests` built three worlds with no world context, no `InitializeActorsForPlay` and no frame guard. All three now get the full pattern, and Resonance's cast resolves correctly in them — the earlier crash was adding play-init WITHOUT a context, which dies in the engine's tick group setup.

`BreakerRotNodeRuntimeTests` was reverted. Giving it a play world makes its ZONES tick, and the test drives zone life by hand with `AdvanceZone`, assuming nothing else advances between its steps; occupancy and follow offsets then move underneath it. That is the test's timing model rather than the world's, and rewriting it is a separate piece of work.

Resonance failed for a completely different and more interesting reason. It detonates the statuses on a target and is paid out of their remaining damage budget. A wind-up burns budget before the detonation collects it, so the identical cast deals measurably less — 405 down to 270 in `MultispellPurchasedRuntime`. That is not a fixture defect; it is the wind-up changing what the ability is worth. Whether Resonance should SNAPSHOT its statuses when the cast begins, the way DoT sources already snapshot, is a ruling and not a number, so it is owed rather than guessed.
