Why this layout
One constellation on the board at a time. The 300px rail carries all six with their invested count, ring progress, and purchasable count, so switching costs one click and nothing is hidden. The class half of this screen is gone — doctrines replaced branches, tiers and investment gates, and they have their own board. Structure comes from horizontal tier bands, not from lines: a node's row IS its tier, and prerequisites are named in the node itself. Zones are 300 / 1220 / 400 with a 44px sub-header on each.
Node anatomy
One chip, four lines, 260 wide. Line one is a 14px type marker plus the name — square Minor, diamond Notable, 2px square Convergence, 2px diamond Keystone, so type is a shape and never a word. Line two is the effect as a number. The base row is rank pips on the left and state on the right, so scanning down a band gives every rank at once. Purchasable is a 2px gold border and gold caption; owned cyan; available neutral 1px; locked drops the fill to bg and dashes the border, with the reason as the caption.
Constellations
Six constellations in the rail, their node bands on the board — one reading model, no second one to learn. Kinesis is drawn as the hub that gates the others, which is a ruling still open rather than a settled layout: a hub privileges one axis, and if that privilege goes, the rail loses its hub tag and the Ring 2 link node becomes an ordinary Convergence. Nothing else changes. Elements stays sealed in suppression teal, the only legal teal on this screen because a rift is an object. Bands divide the 892px board equally rather than carrying a fixed height, so a constellation is always exactly as tall as the screen: 446 at two rings — Volley, Affliction, Bulwark — and 297 at three, which is Precision, Kinesis, and the sealed Elements. Locked bands drop to bg/void with their gate stated in the gutter, so the shape of what is left to buy is visible without hovering.
