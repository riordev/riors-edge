FIELDPLATE / RARITY

The rarity rule, and its second carrier

The enumeration is replaced by a principle, and the principle creates work: if colour can no longer be the only carrier, every rarity needs a mark. Five marks follow, drawn twice — once for a card in a grid, once for a beam seen at distance while moving.

01

Replacement wording

Drop-in, present tense, no site list.
RARITY LAW
Rarity colour appears once per item per view, and never as the only carrier of the fact. A second carrier is a mark, a number, or a word — never a second tint of the same hue.
A key is not an instance: a legend swatch, a filter chip, and the colour helper's own definition name the ramp rather than wearing it, and are always legal. Where a card carries the rail, the name goes to text/primary. Where a beam carries the colour, its height and profile carry the tier as well. Anomalous keeps its full border as the top-rarity exception, because it is also a world object class.
TEST · UI.Rarity.OnceEach — for each item in a rendered view, count the sites painting its rarity hue; a count above one fails, and a site list is never edited to make it pass.
TEST · UI.Rarity.SecondCarrier — desaturate the view; every rarity still identifiable fails nothing, any rarity that vanishes fails.
FALLS OUT AS LEGAL
A card with a coloured rail and a primary-white name. A beam alone in the world. A legend swatch beside its label. A filter chip naming a tier. The Anomalous border. A tooltip that states the tier as a word with no tint at all.
FALLS OUT AS ILLEGAL
A coloured beam under a coloured label. A coloured rail plus a coloured name on one card. A rarity told only by hue, anywhere. Five beams at one height. A screen invented next quarter needs no amendment to be judged: it counts its own sites.
THE SAME FAILURE ELSEWHERE
The four function accents, equip-limit state, and refusal warnings are all bare colour with no second carrier, and they will fail the desaturation test on sight. Each needs the same shape of rule rather than its own list: a function accent names a system, and the system is always also named in words or geometry at the same size. Equip limits already carry a fraction — 3/3 is the second carrier, so the rule is satisfied the moment the fraction is mandatory rather than optional. Refusals need a mark; red alone is not a refusal, a struck cost glyph is.

Why counting, then cutting
The first three marks are strokes counted — one, two, three — because count is the only distinction that survives at 14px without any curve to resolve. The top two stop counting and change silhouette instead: Aberrant loses a corner, Anomalous opens a ring. A grid of loot is read by density in the first glance and by shape only where the player has already stopped to look, which is exactly where the top two tiers matter.
Construction rules
One stroke weight, 1.4px on a 14px box, butt caps, no fill. Strokes sit on whole pixels at 14 and 28; the ring's gap is 4 units wide so it survives to 10px. Marks never rotate, never carry a second hue, and never scale below 12px — below that the rail alone carries the tier and the mark is dropped rather than blurred.

Five beams, distance scale

Heights
95 / 130 / 175 / 235 / 295 cm, replacing the single 180. Each step is roughly 1.35× the last, so adjacent tiers stay separable at a distance where absolute height is unjudgeable and only the ratio between two beams in view can be read. The ramp tops out at 295 — under the 300 standard interior clearance — so no tier clips indoors, which is the one place the cue cannot afford to be the tallest thing in the room.
Profiles
Standard is a plain column. Uncommon adds one collar at half height, Exceptional two. Aberrant breaks: the upper third offsets sideways by its own width. Anomalous separates entirely — a floating cap above a clear gap, the only beam that is not one connected object, which is the same statement its rarity makes.
Two jobs, one language
The card mark and the beam profile are not the same drawing, and should not be: one is read still at 14px, the other moving at 30 m. They share their logic instead — count for the low three, broken silhouette for the top two — so a player who learns one has already learned the other. Beam width stays 30 cm at every tier; only height and profile move.
Two corrections in the mocks I shipped
The inventory masthead carried GEAR SCORE, against the invariant — removed. The affix delta column used triangle glyphs for up and down, which the drawn-geometry rule now forbids and the engine's face could not have rendered anyway — replaced with 6×5 drawn triangles at the same baseline. Both are fixed in the files rather than noted.
